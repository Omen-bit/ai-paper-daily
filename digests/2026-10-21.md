<details>
<summary><b>📖 Abstract (click to expand)</b></summary>

> World models enable embodied agents to anticipate action consequences, yet jointly modeling locomotion and manipulation across diverse embodiments remains challenging due to heterogeneous action spaces and limited multimodal supervision. We present Ropedia W0, the first egocentric loco-manipulation world foundation model spanning humans and robots with diverse embodiments. We curate Xp-Data, comprising 10k hours of egocentric human data and 3k hours of robot data with unified multimodal annotations and systematic quality control. A unified kinematic representation encodes locomotion through camera poses and manipulation through projected skeletons, with robot skeletons combining embodiment-specific forward kinematics with extensive manual keypoint annotation and correction. We also introduce Xp-Teleop, a long-horizon loco-manipulation benchmark based on high-quality dexterous humanoid teleoperation data.

<summary><b>📖 Abstract (click to expand)</b></summary>

> Digital art generation has undergone a massive paradigm shift, moving from static image synthesis to dynamic video generation that can mimic the human painting process. While existing models can synthesize visually stunning time-lapses of paintings being created or "unpainted," they largely treat the creative process as a black box. Current conditioning mechanisms are either too rigid, relying on static geometric cues like Canny edges or depth maps, or too holistic, utilizing simple scalar progress variables that offer no localized control. Consequently, users lack the agency to steer how a painting evolves over time. In this work, we argue that true control over the video generation of artwork requires a joint understanding of two fundamental dimensions: where an artist acts, and when those actions take place.
