<details>
<summary><b>📖 Abstract (click to expand)</b></summary>

> Long-horizon, semi-verifiable agentic tasks often rely on rubric-based evaluation, but the evidence needed to assess these tasks can be spread across the environment state and the execution trace. A judge may overlook this evidence or accept unsupported claims, including an agent's own reports of success. When rubric verdicts serve as reinforcement-learning rewards, such errors can reinforce undesirable behavior. We introduce Sauron, an evidence-first agentic verifier that investigates both state and trace and relies on a claim only after independently validating it against its source. Bounded queries and joint investigation of related criteria reduce context use and redundant work, and each verdict carries an auditable receipt of its supporting evidence.

- 📅 **Date:** 2026-10-18 &nbsp;•&nbsp; 📡 **HuggingFace Papers**
- 🔗 **Links:** [arXiv Abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26848) &nbsp;|&nbsp; [Direct PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.26848) &nbsp;|&nbsp; [HuggingFace Discussion](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.26848)

<details>
<summary><b>📖 Abstract (click to expand)</b></summary>

> In this paper, we systematically investigate the mechanisms, scaling behavior, and generalization of physical Recursive self-improvement. We introduce PhysRSI-eval to quantify physical RSI through a five-axis metric: First-Attempt Performance, Efficiency, Success, Budget and Improvement from failure. Different VLMs are evaluated on 18 manipulation tasks under a fixed interaction budget. The analysis of experimental results motivates the Physical Barrel Hypothesis: different capabilities may constrain different parts of improvement. The five-axis metrics also distinguish systems with close public benchmark averages and show that high scores need not come with early or efficient gains.
